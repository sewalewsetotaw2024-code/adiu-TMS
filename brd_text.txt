Approval Sign-off PAGEREF _Toc226111914 \h 22 Executive Summary ADIU's telecom transport operation is a controlled but highly manual workflow that depends on timely handover of PO/MR documents, work analysis, supplier coordination, QEHS validation, evidence capture, document reconciliation, and closure. The existing SOP defines the core process clearly, but execution still depends on fragmented communication channels, manual evidence collection, and limited real-time operational visibility. The proposed Transport Management System (TMS) will digitize the end-to-end transport lifecycle for telecom implementation materials: request intake, work analysis, quotation and profitability approval, vehicle compliance, dispatch readiness, GPS-based execution monitoring, item-level tracking, incident registration, reconciliation, and final closure. The system will function as both an operational control platform and a management visibility platform. The primary objective is not only automation but also control. The TMS should reduce delivery losses, detect exceptions faster, improve accountability across internal teams and transport suppliers, create a complete audit trail for each transport job, and provide management with credible data on service efficiency, cost leakage, and incident trends. Business intent of the proposed TMS Objective Expected Outcome Operational control Every transport job is executed through a standardized digital workflow with enforced checkpoints and approvals. Visibility Managers can monitor live delivery status, vehicle movement, geofence events, and proof-of-delivery in near real time. Accountability Every action is attributable to a user, role, timestamp, vehicle, and transport job record. Loss reduction Missing, misplaced, stolen, or damaged items are flagged early and investigated through a structured incident workflow. Closure integrity No job is closed without required documents, reconciliation checks, and exception handling. Business Context, Objectives, Scope, and Stakeholders Business Context The governing SOP defines the transport process as a controlled end-to-end flow beginning with vendor/client PO and MR receipt and ending with WCC application, Basecamp update, and portal scan completion. The flow includes formal handover, work analysis, assignment of Transport Officers and Sorting &amp; Delivery Teams, collection of at least three supplier quotations, profitability analysis with variance-based approvals, QEHS compliance gating, time-stamped proof capture, signed MR collection, PO reconciliation, and closure. The TMS must therefore be designed as a workflow-enforcement platform that mirrors this operational sequence and strengthens the control points already defined in the SOP rather than replacing them with an unrelated software process. Project Objectives Digitize the transport process from request registration to closure. Provide real-time delivery visibility through GPS tracking, route status, and geofence monitoring. Track materials, loads, and proof events so discrepancies can be identified quickly. Introduce structured incident management for theft, loss, damage, delay, and compliance breaches. Improve supplier, vehicle, and driver accountability through data-backed execution records. Provide dashboards and reports for operations, finance, security, and executive management. Establish a scalable digital foundation that can later integrate with ERP, finance, warehouse, or vendor systems. In Scope Transport request registration using PO, MR, GRF, site details, and work analysis inputs. Route and trip planning, supplier quotation management, and approval workflow based on variance thresholds. Vehicle and driver onboarding to transport jobs, including QEHS compliance validation. Live GPS tracking and geofence alerts for warehouse, route checkpoints, and project sites. Loading, dispatch, delivery, proof-of-delivery, item confirmation, and last-mile tracking. Item-level discrepancy detection, reconciliation, and exception logging. Incident reporting and case lifecycle management. Role-based access control, dashboards, reports, audit logs, and notifications. Integration layer for Basecamp, GPS service, notifications, and vendor portal updates where feasible. Out of Scope for Initial Release Full enterprise ERP replacement or complete financial accounting functionality. Warehouse management beyond transport-linked staging, loading, and reconciliation activities. Automated bank/payment execution to suppliers. Advanced AI forecasting and optimization engines unless separately approved. Full biometric attendance or HR performance functionality. Key Business Rules to be Enforced by the System Rule ID Rule BR-01 Every transport request must have a unique ID and an initiating source document set (PO, MR, or approved equivalent). BR-02 No work order may be issued before quotation comparison and required approval workflow are completed. BR-03 Where policy requires at least three quotations, an exception approval must exist if fewer are captured. BR-04 Vehicle dispatch is blocked until QEHS compliance validation is approved. BR-05 Loaded quantities and delivered quantities must reconcile to the validated MR or produce a discrepancy record. BR-06 Any missing, damaged, or disputed item must be linked to a discrepancy or incident before transport job closure. BR-07 Closure is blocked if mandatory proof-of-delivery or approved exception evidence is missing. BR-08 Incident case history must be non-editable except through controlled status and comment actions; previous history remains visible. BR-09 User permissions are role-based and cannot be expanded informally outside approved configuration. BR-10 All approvals, rejections, overrides, and escalations must be timestamped and auditable. System Architecture Overview The proposed TMS should follow a modular, service-based architecture that supports both field mobility and centralized control. The design should prioritize traceability, auditability, and controlled integration with existing internal tools and external GPS services. Target solution stack Presentation Layer Mobile application for drivers, Transport Officers, SDTs, and field-confirmation users; responsive web portal for Sourcing, Transport &amp; Warehouse Managers, QEHS, Security, Finance, and Management. Application Layer Workflow engine, transport planning module, supplier/quotation module, item tracking and reconciliation engine, incident management module, dashboards, notification service, and approval engine. Integration Layer GPS provider API, geofencing engine, SMS/email/WhatsApp notifications (where approved), Basecamp integration, vendor portal scan/status update integration, identity provider/SSO if available. Data Layer Transactional database for transport jobs, items, vehicles, GPS events, incidents, approvals, and audit logs; secure file/object storage for proof photos, signed documents, and supporting evidence. Security &amp; Governance Layer Role-based access control, audit logging, encryption, backup, monitoring, and configuration management. Recommended deployment model: secure web-based core platform with Android-first mobile app capability for drivers and field teams, and API-first integration design to allow future connectivity with finance, ERP, warehouse, or vendor systems. Web channel Responsive browser-based portal for operational coordination, approvals, dashboards, and administration. API / service layer RESTful APIs or equivalent service interfaces to support mobile, web, GPS, and third-party integrations. Workflow engine Configurable state transitions, approvals, and notifications without hard-coded dependence on a single process variation. Storage Relational database for structured data plus object/file store for media and signed documents. Observability Application logging, error monitoring, health checks, and operational alerts for support teams. Non-Functional Requirements The TMS will be a control-critical operational system. Non-functional requirements are therefore as important as transactional features because the platform must remain secure, reliable, and usable under field conditions. ID Category Requirement Priority NFR-01 Security The system shall enforce role-based access control, strong password policy or SSO, session timeout, and least-privilege access by role. High NFR-03 Auditability The system shall keep immutable logs of logins, approvals, status changes, incident actions, uploads, and configuration changes. High NFR-07 Scalability The architecture shall support growth in users, trips, sites, vehicles, incidents, and stored evidence without major redesign. High NFR-09 Reliability No approved or captured operational event shall be silently lost; failed sync or integration events must be queued and retried or visibly flagged. High NFR-10 Backup &amp; recovery The solution shall support scheduled backups, media retention, and documented restore procedure with agreed recovery objectives. High NFR-11 Maintainability System modules, rules, and integrations should be designed for maintainability, testability, and controlled change. Medium NFR-12 Compliance The solution shall support internal governance, traceability, and evidence retention expectations for operational audits and investigations. High Security and Access Considerations All users must authenticate individually; shared accounts should not be allowed. Approvals, overrides, and case closures should require explicit user identity and timestamp capture. Evidence files must inherit record-level permissions and should not be downloadable by unauthorized roles. Management users may view aggregated dashboards without necessarily accessing all underlying edit permissions. Administrative changes to approval thresholds, geofence rules, and role permissions should be logged and reviewable. Data Retention and Reliability Expectations Operational records, approvals, incidents, and audit logs should be retained according to company policy and legal/contractual needs. Proof photos, signed MRs, and supporting documents must be stored with durable references and integrity protection. Failed integrations or sync errors must produce visible alerts and retry status instead of disappearing silently. System backups should cover both structured data and evidence files. Benefits and Business Justification The TMS is justified as an operational control investment rather than a convenience tool. It directly addresses transport loss exposure, process inconsistency, weak visibility, delayed exception handling, and limited management reporting. Benefit Area Value to the Organization Reduced losses and disputes Item tracking, proof capture, GPS visibility, and incident linkage reduce the chance that missing or damaged items remain untraceable. Stronger accountability Every request, approval, handover, vehicle assignment, proof event, and case action is attributable to a named user and timestamp. Faster exception response Route deviations, prolonged stoppages, and discrepancy alerts can be escalated immediately rather than discovered after closure attempts. Improved operational efficiency Digital workflows reduce manual follow-up, duplicated communication, and document chasing across teams. Better cost control Quotation comparison, profitability analysis, and variance reporting improve financial discipline and decision-making. Higher management confidence Dashboards and auditable records allow management to approve, monitor, and govern operations with evidence. Scalable digital foundation The platform becomes a base for future integrations with vendor portals, ERP, finance, or warehouse systems. Indicative Business KPIs to Improve After Implementation KPI Improvement Intent On-time delivery rate Improve consistency and reduce uncontrolled delays. Proof completeness rate Increase percentage of jobs closed with complete documentation. Transport cost variance visibility Improve understanding and control of unplanned cost overruns. Supplier performance transparency Enable fact-based supplier selection and escalation.
